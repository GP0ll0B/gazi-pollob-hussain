The singular Bessel kernel Kα and the critical exponent α = 0

Gazi Pollob Hussain constructed a family of singular kernels on ℝ² — Bessel function composed with the Minkowski p-norm, weighted by an inverse power — and proved that at the metric center, kernel and p-derivative diverge together. The threshold between bounded and singular behavior is a single number: α = 0.

01 The Contribution

A single kernel family. A paired singularity. A critical exponent.

Three deliverables, each one checkable. The kernel family Kα; the paired-singularity result Lemma 3; the exact threshold α = 0.

Kα(x, p) = J0( β · Dp(x, μ) ) · Dp(x, μ)−α
What was constructed
Kα — the kernel family J0 composed with the Minkowski p-norm, weighted by an inverse power. A bounded oscillatory factor multiplied by a singular weight. Neither family alone produces this; the composition is the object.
What was proved
Lemma 3 — the paired singularity For α > 0, both the kernel and its p-derivative diverge at the metric center, at matching rate O(D−α). Two chain-rule applications, one logarithmic cancellation, one theorem.
What was identified
α = 0 — the critical exponent The exact value of α where the p-derivative flips from vanishing (bounded kernel) to divergent (singular kernel). Below it, nothing. Above it, everything.
02 Main Result

Lemma 3 — the theorem in one paragraph

The full statement. Below it: the numerical table that confirms every prediction.

Lemma 3. Fix α > 0 and β > 0. For every p > 1:

(i) As x → μ, Kα(x, p) → +∞.

(ii) As x → μ, |∂Kα/∂p| → +∞, with rate O( Dp−α ).

(iii) If α = 0, then |∂K0/∂p| = O( Dp2 ) → 0.

The p-sensitivity diverges at the metric center if and only if the kernel diverges there.

Numerical verification

Central differences in p (step h = 10−3), β = 1, p = 1.75, ray at 30°. Three columns, three regimes. The numbers do exactly what the lemma says they do.

03 Lineage

Built on two centuries of mathematics

Every ingredient of Kα has a documented ancestor. The present contribution sits at the end of a long line, and names what it borrows.

Bessel functions
F. W. Bessel1824
Introduced and studied Jn(z). Established J0(0) = 1 and the recurrence J0′(z) = −J1(z).
p-norms & Minkowski geometry
H. Minkowski1896
Geometrie der Zahlen. Introduced the Lp norm — the level set geometry that Kα inherits through Dp.
Singular integrals & Riesz potentials
M. Riesz1938
Riesz potentials — the prototype of every kernel with power-law singularity. The D−α weight in Kα lives in this tradition.
E. M. Stein1970
Singular Integrals and Differentiability Properties of Functions. The canonical framework for kernels of the form |x|−n+α.
Kernel methods
Broomhead & Lowe · Poggio & Girosi1988 · 1990
Established radial basis function networks — the setting in which the p-norm became a tunable parameter inside a kernel.
Present work
Gazi Pollob Hussain2026
Composed J0 with the Minkowski p-norm and a power weight to form Kα = J0(β·Dp) · Dp−α. Proved Lemma 3: for α > 0, both Kα and |∂Kα/∂p| diverge at the metric center with rate O(D−α). Identified α = 0 as the critical exponent. Uses J0 of Bessel (1824) and the p-norm of Minkowski (1896) as building blocks.

What this contribution claims

The full extent of the claim, stated plainly. Everything below is attributable to Gazi Pollob Hussain, 2026.

✓The kernel family Kα. A specific family of singular kernels on ℝ², formed by composing the Bessel function J0 with the Minkowski p-norm and weighting by an inverse power: Kα(x, p) = J0(β·Dp(x, μ)) · Dp(x, μ)−α, for α ≥ 0 and β > 0.

✓The critical exponent α = 0. Identification of the exact value of α at which the p-derivative transitions from vanishing (bounded kernel, α = 0) to divergent (singular kernel, α > 0). The divergence exists if and only if α > 0.

Glossary

Kα
The singular Bessel kernel: J0(β·Dp) · Dp−α. Singular at the metric center for α > 0.
Dp
Minkowski p-norm distance: (|x−μx|p + |y−μy|p)1/p. Diamond at p = 1, circle at p = 2, square as p → ∞.
Metric center
The point x = μ where Dp(x, μ) = 0. The location of the kernel's singularity.
Critical exponent
α = 0. Where the p-derivative flips from vanishing (bounded kernel) to divergent (singular kernel).
p-sensitivity
|∂Kα/∂p| — the rate of change of the kernel with respect to the norm order p.
Paired singularity
The simultaneous divergence of Kα and |∂Kα/∂p| at the metric center — the content of Lemma 3.

07 Questions

Direct answers

Each question answered in one or two sentences. The clearest form of what this work is.

What is the singular Bessel kernel Kα?
K_α(x, p) = J_0(β · D_p(x, μ)) · D_p(x, μ)^(−α), where J0 is the Bessel function of order zero, Dp is the Minkowski p-norm distance to a fixed center μ, α ≥ 0 is a power exponent, and β > 0 is a spatial frequency. The kernel is a bounded oscillatory factor multiplied by a singular power weight.
What does Lemma 3 state?
For α > 0, both the kernel Kα and its p-derivative |∂Kα/∂p| diverge at the metric center x → μ, at matching asymptotic rate O(D−α). When α = 0, the p-derivative vanishes as O(D²) → 0. The p-sensitivity diverges if and only if the kernel diverges there.
What is the critical exponent?
α = 0. For α > 0 the p-derivative diverges at the metric center. For α = 0 it vanishes. The transition is exact — it happens at a single value of the power parameter.
How is the result verified?
Two ways. (1) An analytic proof via two chain-rule applications, with an explicit cancellation of the logarithmic terms under ray parameterization. (2) A numerical table computed by central differences in p at step h = 10−3, showing the predicted rates D0, D−1, D−2 for α = 0, 1, 2.
